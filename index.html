A source can exist and still be cited wrong.

Cite 3 checks every citation in a text three ways, and we measured how much each check really catches.

  1. 1 Exists?Looked up in Crossref and OpenAlex.
  2. 2 Details right?Title, year and first author against the real record.
  3. 3 Says that?An AI reads the paper's abstract and the sentence that cites it.

Method

We build a labelled test set from real papers (a random sample from OpenAlex), so the right answer is known for every case. Each case is a short text with one citation.

Case typeWhat is wrong
correctNothing. Used to count false alarms.
fabricatedAn invented reference: made-up title, and a made-up DOI in half the cases.
wrong_detailsA real paper with a wrong year, a wrong first author, or a real DOI with another paper's title.
misusedA real paper with right details, but it is about something else than the sentence.
contradictedA real paper with right details, but the sentence says the opposite of its abstract.

Every case is checked once. We then ask what a simpler checker would have flagged from the same run: level 1 only, levels 1 and 2, or all three.

Limits

  • The judge reads the abstract, not the full paper. Some publishers do not share abstracts, so those citations show as "cannot verify" instead of a guess.
  • The test cases are generated from real papers, not collected from real AI answers.
  • The set is small. Read the confidence intervals, not only the percentages.
  • Free-tier models can change. Lookup errors are counted separately and never hidden.
